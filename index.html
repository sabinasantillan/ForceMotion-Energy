Intro — Overview

How They Connect

Force, Motion, and Energy are connected concepts that explain how things move and work. Understanding each one reveals how the universe operates at every scale.

F

Force

A push or pull that can change the movement of an object.

How it works: When force is applied, it changes an object's speed, direction, or shape. Bigger force means bigger movement.

M

Motion

The movement of an object from one place to another.

How it works: Motion occurs when force acts on an object. Cars, bicycles, and even planets move because forces affect them.

E

Energy

The ability to do work or make things happen.

How it works: Energy transfers from one object to another. A moving object has kinetic energy; stored energy is called potential energy.

01 — Force

What is Force?

Force is a push or pull that can change the movement of an object. It is the invisible hand behind every motion in the universe — from the gentle breeze to the roar of a rocket engine.

The word comes from the Latin fortis, meaning "strong." Force is measured in Newtons (N), named after Sir Isaac Newton who first described its laws mathematically.

Push & Pull

Every force is either a push or a pull acting on an object. Kicking a ball is a push; a magnet attracting metal is a pull.

Measured in Newtons

Force is measured in Newtons (N). One Newton is the force needed to accelerate 1 kg of mass at 1 m/s².

Gravity

Gravity is a natural force that pulls objects toward each other. Earth's gravity keeps us on the ground and the Moon in orbit.

01

Force Can Start or Stop Motion

A stationary object stays still until a force acts on it. A moving object keeps moving until a force slows or stops it — this is Newton's First Law of Motion.

02

Force Changes Direction

Force doesn't just start or stop motion — it can also change the direction of a moving object. A soccer player curves a ball by applying force at an angle.

03

Force Can Change Shape

Squeezing clay, stretching a rubber band, or crushing a can — force can deform objects, changing their shape permanently or temporarily.

02 — Motion

What is Motion?

Motion happens when an object changes its position over time. Everything that moves — from a spinning top to a planet orbiting the sun — is in a state of motion.

Motion is described using speed, velocity, and acceleration. It is always caused by a force acting on an object.

Speed & Velocity

Speed is how fast an object moves. Velocity includes direction — a car going 60 km/h north has a different velocity than one going 60 km/h south.

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Pressing the gas pedal accelerates a car; pressing the brake decelerates it.

Types of Motion

Motion can be linear (straight line), circular (around a center), oscillatory (back and forth), or random — like gas molecules bouncing around.

01

Newton's First Law

Law of Inertia

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at the same speed and direction — unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. This is why passengers lurch forward when a car brakes suddenly.

02

Newton's Second Law

Force = Mass × Acceleration

The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass. A heavier object requires more force to achieve the same acceleration as a lighter one. Written as F = ma.

03

Newton's Third Law

Action & Reaction

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. When you push off the ground to jump, the ground pushes back on you with the same force — launching you upward.

03 — Energy

What is Energy?

Energy is the ability to do work or cause movement. It exists in many forms and can transform from one type to another — but it can never be created or destroyed.

This principle is known as the Law of Conservation of Energy — one of the most fundamental laws in all of physics.

Kinetic Energy

Energy of Motion

The energy an object possesses because of its motion. A rolling ball, a flying arrow, a running person — all have kinetic energy. The faster the object moves, the more kinetic energy it has.

Potential Energy

Stored Energy

Energy stored in an object due to its position or condition. A stretched rubber band, a book on a shelf, water behind a dam — all hold potential energy waiting to be released.

01

Energy Can Change Forms

A light bulb converts electrical energy into light and heat. A car engine converts chemical energy (fuel) into mechanical energy (motion). Energy is always transforming.

02

Measured in Joules

Energy is measured in Joules (J). One Joule is the energy needed to apply one Newton of force over one meter of distance.

03

Energy Cannot Be Destroyed

The total energy in a closed system always remains constant. When a ball hits the floor, kinetic energy converts to sound and heat — nothing is lost, only transformed.

04 — Real-Life Applications

Where Do We See It?

Force, motion, and energy are not just textbook concepts — they are alive in every moment of your day, from your morning commute to playing sports after school.

Transportation

Energy from fuel or electricity creates force that makes vehicles move, producing motion that carries people from place to place.

Chemical Energy Mechanical Force Linear Motion

Household Activities

Energy from electricity or the human body applies force in everyday chores — creating motion when cleaning, cooking, and lifting objects.

Electrical Energy Muscular Force Work Done

Sports & Exercise

Energy from food powers the body to apply force, creating motion when running, jumping, throwing, or playing any sport.

Chemical Energy Muscular Force Projectile Motion

Nature — Wind & Water

Natural energy creates force that produces motion — wind moves objects, water flows downhill, and waves reshape the land over time.

Natural Energy Gravitational Force Fluid Motion
